# Committee jurisdiction

The Select Committee on Ethics receives complaints and investigates allegations of improper conduct that may reflect upon the Senate, violations of law, violations of the Senate Code of Official Conduct, and violations of Senate rules and regulations relating to the conduct of Members, officers, and employees of the Senate in the performance of their duties. It makes findings of fact, recommends disciplinary action to the Senate (expulsion, censure, restitution, dismissal, suspension), issues reprimands and letters of admonition, and reports suspected violations of law to Federal and State authorities. It recommends additional Senate rules necessary to ensure proper standards of conduct, renders advisory opinions and interpretative rulings, and conducts ethics education for Members, officers, and employees. Its subject-matter jurisdiction covers the Senate Code of Official Conduct (Standing Rules XXXIV–XLIII), including financial disclosure, gifts, outside earned income, conflicts of interest, blind trusts and waivers; the franking privilege (P.L. 93-191); receipt of foreign gifts and decorations (5 U.S.C. 7342); and unauthorized disclosure of classified intelligence information under Section 8 of S. Res. 400. The Committee has no legislative jurisdiction over any policy area.
